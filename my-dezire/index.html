Finding 1 · the rule that has never run

In August every order your risk engine flagged high-risk was cash on delivery, and every one came back. The rule built to stop that is switched off.

August, with 100% of those parcels tracked end to end: 14 high-risk orders, 14 on cash, 14 returned. September repeated it across 28 orders. Medium risk returns 53.7% of its cash orders and even low risk returns 41.5%. Against 250+ comparable merchants at your basket size, your overall return rate is 37.5% against 7.7% and your cash return rate 47.4% against 19.9%.

Three rules exist in your account. The one that fires today allows cash on every cart. The one that would collect a deposit from the 0.7-and-above risk band — the band above — has never run on a single checkout. And as written it asks for a maximum of ₹99, which is 2.8% of your basket: switching it on is step one, sizing it properly is step two. Finding 3 · the confirmations that don't arrive

Your order-confirmation messages reach 42.6% of the people they are sent to — on a store where eight orders in ten are cash.

Utility messages — order confirmations, updates — should deliver at 85–90% or better. Yours reach 42.6%. This is not a template approval problem: all 50 of your templates are approved and none is paused or rejected. Several flows record sends with no deliveries at all, which points at configuration rather than content.

On a cash-on-delivery business the confirmation message is the main thing standing between an order and a cancellation — and 16.5% of your September orders were cancelled, 18.4% in August. We are not claiming one causes the other; we are saying a message that reaches fewer than half its recipients cannot be doing the job it was built for, and that the link is worth testing first because the fix is configuration, not spend.

₹16,000 / month

At 277 orders a month and a 16.5% cancellation rate, about 46 orders a month are cancelled. If confirmations that actually arrive recover one in five — our assumption — that is 9 orders at your ₹3,567 basket, roughly ₹32,000. We bank half, ₹16,000.

Finding 4 · the prepaid path

Every cash selection on your checkout converts. Only two in three prepaid ones do.

In the week to 12 October, 58 of 58 shoppers who chose cash on delivery placed the order. Of the 22 who chose to pay upfront, only 15 completed. A further 44 of the 124 who reached the payment page chose nothing at all. Your payment success rate is excellent — 96.7%, above benchmark — so this is not a gateway problem.

It matters more here than on most stores, because the path that works flawlessly is also the path that comes back half the time. Every shopper who abandons the prepaid route either leaves or falls back to cash. The levers are the order the options appear in, what a prepaid shopper is offered for choosing it, and a reason to finish for someone already reaching for the back button.

₹10,000 / month

About 190 shoppers a month leave the payment page without choosing, and another 30 choose prepaid and don't finish. One in twenty of that combined group completing — our assumption — is about 11 orders at your ₹3,567 basket, roughly ₹39,000. We bank a quarter, ₹10,000, because some return on their own and some would have ordered on cash anyway.
