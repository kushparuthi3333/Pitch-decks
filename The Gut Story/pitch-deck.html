05 / 15 · Where the headroom is

Six numbers worth a closer look

Half of every order goes out unwatched.

51.8% of your orders are cash-on-delivery, and the risk model that scores every one of them is not connected to anything. It is scoring correctly — orders it flags as high-risk come back at 62% and orders it flags as low-risk come back at 5.6% — so the intelligence exists. There is simply no rule acting on it. That is the largest number on this slide, and the other five are the same story told from different angles: a business converting brilliantly at the front and leaking at the back.

01

Returns run at 21.3%. The peer median is 6.6%.

In July, 721 of 3,937 orders came back. Cash-on-delivery returns run 41.6% against a peer median of 27.9%, and it is accelerating — 17.1% → 21.3% in a single month. Prepaid returns are 1.5% and cancellations are 0.4%, so this is entirely a cash-on-delivery problem and entirely after the parcel leaves you.

Risk-tier analysis · July 2026, the last settled month

02

The pack ladder is priced backwards

Your 30-capsule pack works out at ₹41.30 a capsule. The 60-pack works out at ₹46.63 — 12.9% more per capsule for buying more — and the page labels it “save 1%”. A shopper doing the arithmetic is correctly told that trading up is a worse deal. It is your second-best seller anyway.

Live pack pricing & on-page badges · 26 Aug 2026

03

Your best offer is labelled as your worst

Buy the 60-pack and you get a 30-pack free. That is 90 capsules for ₹2,798 — ₹31.09 a capsule, comfortably the best price anywhere on the store. The badge next to it reads “save 1%”. The mechanic is live and working — 600 free units shipped last month — it is only the arithmetic on the page that is fighting it.

The word “subscribe” appears zero times on your homepage or product page, on a hero SKU literally named “Pack Of 30 Capsules”. Returning buyers already spend +18.6% more per order.

05

₹85L a month of paid traffic, and no ROAS exists

99.88% of your orders — 5,768 of 5,775 — carry no attributed channel at all, while 79.9% of your sessions arrive from paid social. The cause is a naming convention: the creative name is being written into the medium field, so every iteration mints a new campaign. The tagging isn't random — it encodes funnel stage, offer, creator and week. It just isn't in the right fields. Cost is also recorded on 0% of sales, so there is no break-even either.

1,870 shoppers a month reach checkout and never type a number

At your own new-customer rate that is roughly 1,190 orders a month. The same pattern shows up in your best channel: WhatsApp adds to cart at 26.3% — 2.9× your site average, the best source you have — and then has the lowest checkout conversion of any channel, held back at exactly this step.
