11 / 13 · Your diagnosis

Specific to you, not to your category

What we've already seen in your business.

We ran your GoKwik account and your storefront against the network before this meeting. Four findings — the three we can put a number on, and one we deliberately cannot, because until it is fixed nobody can honestly size it.

Finding 1 · the big one

Roughly 10,400 shoppers a month open your checkout and never type a phone number — and we can tell you which traffic they arrive on.

That is 29% of every checkout session, and they convert at zero. It is not an OTP fault: your OTP delivers 100% of the time and succeeds 99.2%. These shoppers never reach the OTP at all. It is why your login conversion is 68.4% against an 80.5% benchmark — the single largest gap anywhere in your funnel.

And here is the part that turns it from a mystery into a job. Your own re-engagement messaging is your single largest traffic source — 7,257 sessions a month, one session in five — and it converts at 6.9% against your 30.7% average. The entire loss is at one step: those shoppers reach the address page 42% of the time against roughly 77% for every other source. Once past login they behave normally — their address step performs at par. This is not a campaign-quality problem. It is a plumbing problem: your own messages are handing shoppers to a checkout that does not recognise them.

₹11.9L / month

Recovering half the gap on that traffic alone — 6.9% to 15.4% — is 617 orders a month. Cross-checked a second way: the network's measured +400bps on login conversion, applied across all 35,298 sessions, gives ₹12.2L. Two independent methods, landing within 3% of each other.

Finding 2 · basket

Seven orders in ten sit in one ₹1,000–2,000 band — and your offer ladder is aimed correctly but published three different ways.

7,636 orders a month land between ₹1,000 and ₹2,000 at an average of ₹1,485. That is 72% of your orders and 55% of your revenue. It mirrors your catalogue exactly — 83% of your 1,102 variants are priced in that same band. This is a merchandising ceiling, not a shopper preference.

✅ And you have already done the hard part. Almost every brand we audit sets its offer thresholds below its average basket, where they ask the shopper for nothing. Yours sit above it — ₹1,999, ₹2,199, buy-2 and buy-3 — which is exactly right. Two things stop it working. The same ₹545 free-gift offer is published at ₹1,899, ₹1,999 and ₹2,199 on three different surfaces, with your ₹1,935 basket sitting between them. And none of it appears on a single product page — it lives in the announcement bar, the footer and the cart, all of which are after or away from the decision.

₹15.4L / month

Checkout upsell at the network's measured +750bps on AOV. Cross-check on your own numbers: moving a quarter of that ₹1–2K band into your next band — ₹1,485 to ₹2,493 — is ₹19.2L. We have taken the lower of the two. And it pays twice: cash-on-delivery falls from 72.3% in that band to 0% above ₹10,000, so a bigger basket is also a prepaid one.

Finding 3 · the payment screen

10,001 shoppers a month reach your payment screen and choose nothing — and six of your seven checkout rules depend on the step that loses them.

Your pay-select conversion is 54.4% against a 60.8% benchmark. It is not a payments problem — UPI succeeds 98.4% of the time against a 65–75% benchmark, your blended success rate is 95.1%, and your failed-payment rate is 0.03% against a category 0.81%. Everything that gets chosen, works. It is the choosing that stalls.

There is one specific, fixable cause inside it, and it is worth its own line. Six of your seven checkout rules push shoppers into part-prepaid COD — and only 46.9% of the people who select it go on to attempt payment, against a 60.5% benchmark. More than half the shoppers your own rules route there drop at the confirmation step. That single fix improves six rules at once. ✅ And the rules themselves are working: every part-prepaid COD rule you run converts its segment to 100% prepaid.

₹12.3L / month

Closing half the 6.4-point gap on the 21,932 sessions that reach your payment screen, at your own 90.7% payment success rate and ₹1,935 basket. We have banked half because some of these shoppers overlap with Finding 1 — a rescued login session also has to pass this screen.

Finding 4 · the blind spot

Your courier reports back on one order in twenty — so your returns rate is not a number, and we won't pretend otherwise.

In August, 10,181 of your 10,870 orders had no final delivery status recorded — a 5.2% fill rate. July, which has had time to settle, reached only 18.8%. Returns currently read 11.3% against a peer median of 8.3%, but that is computed on the fraction of orders that reported back. It is a floor, not a measurement — the true figure can only be higher.

GoKwik is already cutting returns 2.8 points and cash-on-delivery 3.4 points against your own non-GoKwik baseline. The sequence is not negotiable: fix the feed, measure, then ladder COD. Not the other way round.
