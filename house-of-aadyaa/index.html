We ran your store, your Shopify admin and your GoKwik account against the network before this meeting — more than seventy questions in all. Four findings: the three we can put a number on, and one we deliberately cannot yet, because your ad account is not connected.

Finding 1 · the first screen

Three in ten of your checkout sessions never identify a shopper.

4,052 checkout sessions in the last 30 days. About 1,200 of them — 29.5% — never resolve to a known shopper, and they convert at 0.6%. 585 of those sessions never get as far as typing a phone number. Login runs 69.5% against a 77.7% category median, and the gap is almost entirely on the website: 62.4% there, against 89.1% in your app. It is not the SMS — all 670 OTPs sent last month were delivered and verified. GoKwik's own read is that the shopper's identity signal is not reaching checkout for these sessions, and that is ours to trace.

The pattern is sharpest on cold traffic. Shoppers arriving from Google get past login 34% of the time, and from Google Shopping 24%, against 91–98% for your Facebook and Instagram visitors — and past the login screen your checkout is genuinely good: address 97.9% against 95.7%, with 97.8% of sessions arriving pre-filled. Three checkout features the network rates “enable now” for jewellery brands are off: an exit offer on checkout (+90bps), one-click recommended coupon (+65bps) and per-item delivery dates (+50bps).

₹4.1L / month

Closing half the login gap to the category median is +4.1 points of login on 4,052 sessions — 166 more shoppers past the first screen, converting at your 38.8% post-login rate — +64 orders, ₹3.1L at your ₹4,845 checkout basket. The three features are 205bps on 4,052 sessions — +83 orders, ₹4.0L — of which we bank half, ₹2.0L. That is ₹5.1L, and we have taken a further 20% off for overlap, because a shopper rescued at login also sees the exit offer.

Finding 2 · payments

39 payments were debited last month without an order being created — ₹2.86L refunded.

This one is on our side of the table, and we would rather say so first. 3.57% of payment attempts on your checkout were debited without an order being created — seventeen times the 0.21% for your category. GoKwik refunded 38 of those orders automatically, ₹2,85,741 in total — about ₹7,520 each, well above your average basket, so it is falling on your larger orders. By method: UPI 29 orders (3.47%), card 7 (4.24%), net-banking 2, pay-later 1.

Alongside it, card payments succeed 78.2% of the time against an 86.3% category median, on the method that carries your biggest baskets — card orders average ₹7,515. And UPI, 72% of your online revenue, runs 93.9% against a 96.8% median. The reason code is missing on almost every one of these failures, which is itself the first thing we fix: the order-level report tells us whether it is a gateway confirming late or an order being rejected, and those go to two different teams.

₹1.4L / month

We bank half of the ₹2.86L, because some of those customers will already have paid again once refunded. The card gap we have not sized at all. The part we would not put a number on is the customer who was charged, got no order, and did not come back.

Finding 3 · the basket

Half your online orders stop short of the step you already offer.

Your basket is already strong — the 89th percentile of Shopify brands on our network, about twice the category median. And your offer ladder is already built: 10% off everything, and 15% above ₹4,999. But 490 of your 998 online orders last month landed between ₹2,000 and ₹5,000, at an average of ₹3,383 paid. Meanwhile the 89 orders above ₹10,000 brought ₹16.2L — almost as much as those 490.

Nothing on the path tells a ₹3,383 shopper how close they are to the next step. 98.9% of your website orders already carry a discount, at 21.7% of gross sales, so going deeper is not the answer — climbing is. A “frequently bought together” block is live on every product page and website orders still average 1.6 pieces, on a catalogue made of pieces that are worn together. And the next rung is invisible too: your shipping policy makes delivery free on prepaid orders from ₹4,000, while your FAQ and your terms each describe free shipping differently.

₹1.6L / month

Moving one in five of those 490 mid-band orders to ₹5,000 is +₹1,617 each, ₹1.58L a month — measured on what the shopper actually paid, so the extra discount above ₹4,999 is already taken out. No change to your prices.

Finding 4 · traffic

30% of your website visits come from one source — and they almost never shop.

Your Shopify admin records 429,117 website sessions in the last 30 days. 129,559 of them carry the tag ig / paid. They add to cart 0.13% of the time and stay an average of eleven seconds. Visits tagged facebook / paid add to cart at 1.87% and stay 45 seconds; your Instagram feed placements at 3.5%, your Facebook feed at 5.5%. One campaign alone sent 29,962 visits to your men's collection at an average of three seconds and a 0.02% add-to-cart rate — and that page is fine: it renders 16 products, 13 in stock, at ₹2,100, and it isn't in your main navigation, so those visits were sent there directly. We are careful about what this does and doesn't prove. And we do not know the cause: a traffic-objective campaign, a placement that invites accidental taps, or an audience the creative doesn't match would all look like this — and some of it lands on your bridal Tanmani range, a considered ₹32,500 purchase that may well close in your stores or on WhatsApp. What it does prove is that about a third of the visits you are paying for are barely being looked at.
